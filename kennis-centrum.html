Netherlands

Filed under Expat Tax → Netherlands. Full detail lives on the Netherlands tax guide.

Do I need to file US taxes if I live in the Netherlands?

The US taxes citizens and green card holders on worldwide income regardless of residence. In most cases little or no tax is owed, but the filing obligation itself doesn't go away.

Read more →

How does the Dutch "box" system affect my US tax return?

Box 1, 2, and 3 each tax differently — and each interacts with US reporting in its own way, particularly Box 3's deemed‑return approach to savings and investments.

Read more →

Does the 30%‑ruling affect my US taxes?

The ruling changes your Dutch taxable income, which needs to be factored correctly into your US return — it doesn't change whether you need to file.

Read more →

I run a Dutch BV — what do I need to report to the US?

Owning or co‑owning a Dutch BV, or acting as a DGA, triggers US foreign‑corporation reporting requirements a Dutch‑only accountant typically won't flag.

Read more →

How do I avoid double taxation as an American in the Netherlands?

The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion and Foreign Tax Credit are the two main tools — which one fits depends on your income type, with the US–NL treaty adding a further layer for pensions and social security.

Read more →

Do I need to file an FBAR for my Dutch bank accounts?

If your combined non‑US account balances exceeded $10,000 at any point in the year, yes — and it's also why Dutch banks ask US persons for tax details under FATCA.

Read more →

What are the filing deadlines for the Netherlands and the US?

Dutch and US tax years both run January–December, but deadlines and extensions differ — worth tracking both separately.

Read more →

What happens if I've never filed a US return before?

The Streamlined Procedure exists to bring you current penalty‑free in most cases — but generally only if you act before the IRS contacts you.

United Kingdom

Filed under Expat Tax → United Kingdom. Full detail lives on the UK tax guide.

Do I need to file US taxes if I live in the UK?

The US taxes citizens and green card holders on worldwide income regardless of residence. UK tax paid usually credits against US tax owed, but the filing obligation itself doesn't go away.

Read more →

How is my Stocks & Shares ISA taxed by the US?

An ISA is tax‑free in the UK but not recognised as such by the IRS — income and gains inside it are generally still reportable.

Read more →

What is a PFIC and does it affect my UK investments?

Many UK mutual funds and investment trusts are classed as PFICs for US purposes — an easy‑to‑miss source of unexpectedly punitive US tax treatment.

Read more →

I have a SIPP — how is it treated for US tax purposes?

SIPPs and workplace pensions carry their own US reporting requirements and interact with the US–UK treaty's pension provisions.

Read more →

Why do US and UK advisors sometimes disagree on my tax treatment?

Each is usually optimizing for one side of the picture — the US–UK tax treaty is what reconciles the two.

Read more →

Why does my UK bank ask about my US tax status?

That's FATCA — a reporting requirement on the institution's side, not a sign of a problem on yours.

Read more →

What are the filing deadlines for the UK and the US?

UK Self Assessment and the US tax year run on different calendars — both deadlines need tracking separately.

Read more →

I'm self‑employed or run a Ltd company — what do I need to report?

Sole traders and UK Ltd company owners face US reporting implications a UK‑only accountant typically won't flag.
